A practical guide to crypto market signals

Crypto Master AI is a cryptocurrency market-research tool. It presents scanner outputs to help visitors review market conditions; it cannot predict the future or remove the risk of loss. Use the explanations below to understand the information shown before making independent decisions.

What is a LONG signal?

A LONG label means the scanner's configured rules currently lean toward a bullish setup. It is not a command to buy, and it does not mean the price must rise. Check the live market, liquidity, spread, news and your own risk limits.

What is a SHORT signal?

A SHORT label means the configured rules currently lean toward a bearish setup. Short positions can involve leverage, borrowing, funding charges and liquidation risk. Some products may not be available or suitable in every jurisdiction.

When does NO TRADE matter?

NO TRADE means the scanner has not identified a setup that meets its current rules. Waiting is a valid research outcome. Forcing a trade when signals conflict or market data is incomplete can increase unnecessary risk.

How should confidence be read?

A confidence or score value describes the model's internal scoring, not a verified chance of profit. Unless independently calibrated and validated, a score of 80 must not be read as an 80% chance that a trade will win.

Understanding Stop-Loss and Take-Profit

A Stop-Loss (SL) is a planned level for limiting a position's loss; a Take-Profit (TP) is a planned level for taking gains. Scanner levels are reference estimates, not guaranteed execution prices. Fast markets, exchange outages, gaps, spread, trading fees, funding and slippage can cause actual results to differ. A stop order may fill at a worse price than expected.

Basic risk-management checklist

  • Decide the maximum loss you can tolerate before considering a position.
  • Understand position size, fees, leverage, liquidation price and order types.
  • Avoid risking rent, emergency savings, borrowed money or money needed for essential expenses.
  • Check the data timestamp, exchange and liquidity; stale or missing data can invalidate a signal.
  • Keep a journal and test a method across different market conditions, including fees and slippage.

Why backtests can be misleading

Historical results depend on data quality and assumptions. Missing candles, survivorship bias, overfitting, unrealistic fills, fees and slippage can make a backtest look better than real trading. Paper trading can help test an operational workflow, but it still cannot reproduce every real-market condition. Past performance does not predict future results.

Our limitations and transparency

Market data may be delayed, unavailable or incomplete because of provider outages, rate limits or exchange restrictions. Model outputs can be wrong, react late or perform poorly in new market conditions. Crypto Master AI does not guarantee accuracy, returns, uninterrupted access or profitable signals. The dashboard is for research and education, not personalised financial, investment, tax or legal advice.
